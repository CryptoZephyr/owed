## The promise, with numbers

An employer promises every candidate they interview an answer by a set date, and backs that promise with money held in PayPal.

<Receipt label="The promise, with numbers">
  <Line label="The employer promises" value="$25 per candidate, reply by a set date" />
  <Line label="PayPal holds" value="$75 for 3 candidates, nothing charged yet" />
  <Line label="Candidate hears back in time" value="Nothing is charged" />
  <Line label="Date passes with no answer" value="Owed pays that candidate $25" />
  <Line label="Role closes" value="PayPal releases the rest" total />
</Receipt>

AI helps the employer write the reply. It never decides who gets paid. Fixed rules on the server move the money.

<Callout title="Proven in the PayPal sandbox">
  On the live demo, a candidate's date passed with no answer, and Owed paid them $1.00 through PayPal without anyone pressing a button. Owed runs on the PayPal sandbox only, so all money is test money.
</Callout>

[Try the live demo](https://owed-vert-ten.vercel.app) or [see the evidence](/docs/proof/verified-sandbox-transactions).

## Who it's for

Employers who interview candidates and want their promise to reply to mean something. Candidates, who see the amount and the date before they accept a private invitation.

## The problem

Candidates prepare, take time off work and sit through several rounds, then often hear nothing. 63% of US job candidates say they were left in the dark after an interview ([Greenhouse 2025 Workforce & Hiring Report](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/only-7-of-candidates-say-the-job-market-favors-them-amidst-challenges-of-ageism-ai-cheating-and-ghosting-302507370.html)). Most employers mean to reply, but nothing happens to them when they forget.

## The rule Owed guarantees

Every interviewed candidate hears back by the set date, or gets paid for their time through PayPal.

## What Owed can't show yet

Owed is a working PayPal sandbox demo. It hasn't run with live PayPal or been tested with real employers.
